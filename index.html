Publications

In Situ Growth and Regeneration of Photocatalyst: A Continuous High-Throughput Microphotoreactor

Langmuir • 2026

Authors: Sunil Kumar Singh, Suhanya Duraiswamy
A process for high-throughput and continuous long-term photocatalytic operations using a microphotoreactor (MPR) is introduced. This is the first process of its kind that uses an MPR for long-term operations without dismantling it for catalyst regeneration and that can be directly installed in industry. We used a well-known and versatile photocatalyst, ZnO, for this process since the catalyst growth, properties, and mechanism of photocatalytic activity are well-studied and documented. ZnO nanorods grow in situ after assembling the reactor by flowing precursors using the seed-mediated hydrothermal growth method. We demonstrate that our process and hence the developed MPR is facile, robust, and reproducible using two model systems to validate photocatalytic activity: (1) degradation of an organic dye and (2) degradation of antibiotics and their combinations.

Microbiosensors: Advancing diagnosis and prognosis through MEMS

Biomicrofluidics • 2025

Authors: Sushama Agarwalla, Sunil Kumar Singh, Suhanya Duraiswamy
Microbiosensors are miniaturized, high-performance analytical devices that integrate biorecognition elements with microscale transducers for the rapid and sensitive detection of biological analytes. The integration of microelectromechanical systems, nanomaterials, and microfluidic platforms has significantly advanced the capabilities of these sensors, enabling non-invasive, portable, and real-time monitoring. This review focuses specifically on the role of microbiosensors in healthcare diagnostics, with emphasis on the detection of infectious agents and biomarkers associated with these diseases. We discuss recent developments in optical and electrochemical microbiosensors, highlight their application in continuous physiological monitoring, and explore their potential in personalized medicine.

A traveling surface acoustic wave-based micropiezoactuator: A tool for additive-and label-free cell lysis

Biomicrofluidics • 2024

Authors: Sushama Agarwalla, Sunil Kumar Singh, Suhanya Duraiswamy
We propose a traveling surface acoustic wave (TSAW)-based microfluidic method for cell lysis that enables lysis of any biological entity, without the need for additional additives. Lysis of cells in the sample solution flowing through a poly(dimethyl siloxane) microchannel is enabled by the interaction of cells with TSAWs propagated from gold interdigitated transducers (IDTs) patterned onto a LiNbO3 piezoelectric substrate, onto which the microchannel was also bonded. Numerical simulations to determine the wave propagation intensities with varying parameters including IDT design, supply voltage, and distance of the channel from the IDT were performed. Experiments were then used to validate the simulations and the best lysis parameters were used to maximize the nucleic acid/protein extraction efficiency (> 95%) within few seconds.

PDF https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0209663

Effect of Nozzle Configuration on Performance of a Spray Dryer

Journal of Chemical Technology and Metallurgy • 2024

Authors: Ashraf Ali Basheer, Sunil Kumar Singh
In this work, hydrodynamics and drying characteristics of spray dryer is numerically investigated using computational fluid dynamics (CFD) using Euler-Lagrangian (EL) approach. The gas phase is modelled as the continuous phase and solid particle as the dispersed phase. The turbulence in the gas phase is predicted using RNG version of k-ε model. As air flow pattern influences the time spent by particle in drying chamber, the spatial variation of air velocity and its circulation rate is quantified. Accordingly, optimum conditions for drying the feed slurry are determined. Further, five different outlet pipe locations are chosen and the optimum location is identified which supports the highest evaporation rate. To improve the product quality, conventional nozzle is modified and particle impact positions are analyzed. The particles impact positions on the dryer’s surface are found to be minimum for the proposed nozzle configuration and it improves the final product quality.

PDF https://doi.org/10.59957/jctm.v59.i1.2024.14

Interplay Between Quorum Sensing, Biofilm Formation and Antibiotic Resistance in Staphylococcus Aureus

IntechOpen • 2026

Authors: Sushama Agarwalla, Sunil Kumar Singh
Staphylococcus aureus is a versatile Gram-positive bacterium that can colonize human skin and mucosal surfaces without causing damage, yet it is capable of inducing a broad range of infections, from minor skin abscesses to serious conditions such as sepsis and endocarditis. Its pathogenic efficacy resides in its ability to adapt dynamically to environmental signals via intricate regulatory networks. Two primary factors influencing this adaptability are quorum sensing (QS) and biofilm formation, which collectively coordinate the bacterium’s shift between planktonic and sessile states. This chapter provides a comprehensive overview of the molecular interactions among QS, biofilm development, and antibiotic resistance in S. aureus.

Flow Visualization Techniques and Applications: Insights from Particle Tracking

CRC Press • 2026

Authors: Sunil Kumar Singh, Sushama Agarwalla, Deepak Kumar Agarwalla
Flow visualization and particle tracking are essential for comprehending and examining fluid flow behavior in a wide range of applications, from engineering to biological systems. This study overviews the principles, methodologies, and uses of flow visualization and particle tracking techniques. Further, we explore various visualization methods, including label and label-free tracking techniques such as dye injection, laser-induced fluorescence, smoke flow visualization, Schlieren imaging, etc., emphasizing their benefits and limitations. In addition to exploring the use of particle tracking techniques in the analysis of fluid dynamics, biofluid mechanics, and other fields, the study examines particle tracking methods like particle image velocimetry (PIV) and clarifies the principles underlying their operation. This highlights the significance of these techniques in the advancement of research and technology.

John Wiley & Sons, Inc. • 2025

Authors: Sushama Agarwalla, Sunil Kumar Singh, Mohammed Adil Ibrahim, Suhanya Duraiswamy
A secure work environment requires prioritizing safety management, identifying potential hazards and conducting thorough risk assessments. A comprehensive safety management system is essential, which defines workplace safety policies, procedures and roles. Hazard identification is crucial, and tools such as job safety analysis and safety data sheets are used to identify potential risks. This chapter discusses hazard identification approaches, including certain aspects of risk assessment. It explores the ideas behind Layer of Protection Analysis (LOPA) and emphasize how quantitative risk analysis and LOPA relate to one another. Identifying risks in the chemical industry is an essential step in safeguarding the well-being of individuals, the surrounding community and the natural environment. A process hazards checklist is a thorough inventory of potential issues and areas that need careful examination. A commonly used method for identifying safety issues in laboratory and process areas and finding solutions is safety reviews.

Antimicrobial coatings: current mechanisms, challenges, and opportunities

John Wiley & Sons, Inc. • 2024

Authors: Sushama Agarwalla, Sunil Kumar Singh, Mohammed Adil Ibrahim, Hemanth Noothalapati, Suhanya Duraiswamy
Coatings come in various configurations (organic, inorganic, and polymer) and can protect many kinds of surfaces related to architectural, industrial, or healthcare settings. The composite materials used to create coatings each bring their own set of benefits to the table. Their existence changes the surface's aesthetics and functionality. Antimicrobial coatings, as the name indicates, are protective coatings that shield and prevent microbial activity on any surface. Antimicrobial coatings’ capacity to safeguard surfaces and people contributes to their rising popularity, especially after the pandemic the world has been through. COVID-19. Microbes, bacteria, viruses, or fungi, can spread from person to person via any number of routes, including direct contact with surfaces, equipment, walls, fabrics, and food. It is not always possible to clean, disinfect, sanitize, or treat surfaces with solid chemicals to stop their growth; instead, the ideal solution is to use coatings that inhibit their growth.
